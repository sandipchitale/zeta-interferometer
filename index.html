The Zeta-Zeros Interferometer

A diffraction grating with one slit at each non-trivial zero of ζ(s). Plane-wave light in → the far-field fringes land on the primes.

The idea

The non-trivial zeros ½ + iγn and the primes are Fourier-dual (Riemann's explicit formula). A grating is an optical Fourier transform: under parallel illumination the screen shows | 𝓕{slits} |².

Put a slit at each height γn and the bright fringes appear at v = log pk — the logarithms of the prime powers.

Reading the screen
  • Centre fringe v=0 → label 1 (the zeroth order)
  • Upper wing → primes p at v=log p
  • Faint marks → prime powers pk (toggle)
  • Lower wing → the mirror, labelled 1/p (conjugate ±γ symmetry)
Layout
  • Grating = the critical line, Re(s) = ½ — a plane ∥ the YZ plane with a slit at each zero height γn.
  • Horizontal = Re(s), vertical = Im(s) = t; the Y axis is Re = 0.
  • Line source at right (x→+∞); the curved screen at left shows the far-field pattern.
Try it

Drag “Zero pairs” (or press ←/→). With a few zeros the pattern is fuzzy; as you add more zeros the prime fringes sharpen and lock onto the labels — the explicit formula converging before your eyes.

Honest caveats
  • Spots sit at prime powers (so 4=2², 8=2³, 9=3²… appear too).
  • Few zeros ⇒ broad, noisy peaks; needs many to resolve large primes.
  • Screen axis is logarithmic in the prime coordinate; uniform slits.
